Dipogon

A percussion synth where every key on the keyboard holds its own drum sound. Roll new sounds, glide and morph from key to key, and shape the whole kit with 8 macro knobs.

Play in browser

Free, forever.

Dipogon running in the browser. The plugin is the same panel.

What it is

Play a key, turn a knob, and the change stays on that key. One keyboard becomes a whole kit, and every key can be something different. It runs in your browser and as a plugin: VST3 and AU on a Mac, VST3 on Windows and Linux.

  • Every key, its own soundEach key keeps its own drum. Don’t like one? Roll a new sound for it, or deal a whole new kit.
  • Glide and morphSlide and morph from one key’s sound to the next.
  • 8 macro knobsShape everything at once. MIDI-map them to your controller and automate them from your DAW.
  • Sound sourcesWavetable oscillators, FM, noise and a click layer.
  • ShapingFilters with a live curve display, and drive.
  • EffectsEcho and reverb.
  • ModulationPer-knob modulation, with a step sequencer.

How it came about

The idea comes from AFX mode on the Novation Bass Station II, where every key can hold its own sound. I wanted that idea as a full drum synth.

Built on the Sonic Potions LXR

Dipogon’s drum voices come from the Sonic Potions LXR, an open-source DIY drum machine designed by Julian Schmidt. Its engine is ported from the LXR firmware, as carried on in the LXR-02. Huge thanks to Sonic Potions for making it open.

Dipogon is unofficial: it isn’t made, approved or endorsed by Sonic Potions or Erica Synths. “LXR” and “LXR-02” are trademarks of Erica Synths.

Install the plugin

Free, for Mac, Windows and Linux. Pick your computer below, then rescan plugins in your DAW. It appears as an instrument called Dipogon.

Your computer

macOS

Apple Silicon and Intel · VST3, AU and app

Paste into Terminal

curl -fsSL https://github.com/johnnyhawk02/lxr-afx/releases/download/plugin-latest/install.sh | bash

Open Terminal (Cmd + Space, type Terminal), paste, press Return. It installs the VST3, the AU and the app. Run it again any time to update.

Why Terminal and not a download? I don’t have a paid Apple developer account, so the plugin isn’t signed by Apple. If you download it in a browser, macOS blocks it. Fetched from Terminal it installs without any fuss. The script is short, so feel free to read it first.

Your computer

Windows

64-bit Windows 10 and 11 · VST3 New · beta

VST3 zip App zip

Unzip it and put the Dipogon.vst3 folder in C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, then rescan plugins.

Windows may warn that it’s from an unknown publisher. Click More info, then choose Run anyway.

Your computer

Linux

x86_64 · VST3 New · beta

VST3 zip App zip

Unzip it into ~/.vst3, then rescan plugins.

It needs WebKitGTK. On Debian or Ubuntu: sudo apt install libwebkit2gtk-4.1-0

The Windows and Linux builds are brand new and haven’t been tried in many DAWs yet. If something goes wrong, email hello@johnnyhawk.org and say which computer and DAW you have.

Then rescan in your DAW

  • Ableton LiveSettings > Plug-ins, turn on “Use VST3 Plug-in System Folders”, click Rescan.
  • Logic Pro / GarageBand(Mac) It appears under Audio Units. If not, restart Logic.
  • FL StudioOptions > Manage plugins > Find plugins.
  • ReaperOptions > Preferences > Plug-ins > VST > Re-scan.
  • Bitwig, Cubase, Studio One and othersRescan plugins or restart the DAW.
